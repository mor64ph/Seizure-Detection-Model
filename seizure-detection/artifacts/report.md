**Event sensitivity and FA/h are pooled** (total detected over total seizures, total alarms over total interictal hours), so they read as "detected N of M seizures". PR-AUC and recall are means across folds. Precision is given **both** ways, because the two disagree by more than an order of magnitude: the fold mean weights a subject with a handful of high-precision windows equally with one emitting tens of thousands of false positives, while the pooled figure answers "of everything flagged, how much was real". Quote the pooled one.

**time in alarm** is the fraction of evaluated recording time spent inside an alarm, and it exists to close a loophole in FA/h: false alarms are counted per run, so a detector that never stops alarming scores one long false alarm and an excellent FA/h. The `dummy` row is the proof — it predicts the majority class for every window, so it alarms continuously, and without this column it appears to detect every seizure at a competitive false-alarm rate. A usable detector keeps time in alarm small.

## Feature views compared (PRD §8.3)

| model | aggregated LOSO | per-channel LOSO | LOSO winner | aggregated Δ | per-channel Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hgb | 0.205 | 0.308 | per_channel | 0.182 | 0.404 |
| logreg | 0.164 | 0.134 | aggregated | -0.095 | 0.029 |
| rf | 0.178 | 0.301 | per_channel | 0.161 | 0.250 |

Δ is the random-split inflation: random PR-AUC minus LOSO PR-AUC.

The PRD predicted the aggregated view would beat per-channel under LOSO while losing under a random split, on the reasoning that per-channel capacity gets spent on subject identity. **The first half is falsified and the second is confirmed.** Per-channel wins under LOSO for every model, so channel-specific information is genuinely useful for detecting seizures — but its inflation Δ is larger than the aggregated view's in every case, so it is also genuinely more subject-specific. The aggregated view is more *robust*; the per-channel view is more *accurate* under honest evaluation. Both can be true at once and the PRD's framing assumed they could not.

The sharpest evidence sits in the linear model. Logistic regression shows essentially no inflation on the 56 aggregated features and substantial inflation on the 252 per-channel ones. A single hyperplane has the same capacity in both cases, so the leak cannot be attributed to model capacity alone — it lives in the channel-specific features themselves.
